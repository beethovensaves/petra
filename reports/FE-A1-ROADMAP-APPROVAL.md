## What the implemented experience must do

The left panel is a normal conversation: question, answer, follow-up, cited
objects and a persistent composer. Plans, progress and required decisions are
contextual parts of that conversation. Empty Projects still support outcome
framing, definition review, clarification, material intake and a grounded
no-evidence response. They never use a substitute Project or invented graph.

AG-UI transports validated interaction state through the official adapters.
It does not confer command authority. The Workspace reacts to the exact turn,
result or direct selection while retaining drafts and task identity. The
default view exposes the outcome, actual progress and next required decision;
Explanation, Basis, Control and Graph/History disclose deeper records within
the same task. Required warnings and consequential decisions remain visible.

Native examination shares identity, scope, basis, provenance, uncertainty,
comparison, validation and eligible-action slots. Scientific Claim/Assertion
and Hypothesis meanings remain scientific; controlled-writing Requirement and
Interpretation retain textual basis and competing readings. The proposed third
definition is **event planning**, with native requirements, constraints and
options examined against supplied evidence. This does not add booking,
purchasing, external scheduling or automatic expenditure to A1.

The graph displays recorded objects, relations, rivals, dependencies and
obligations, with exact snapshots, layers, omissions and projection freshness.
Selecting an object updates its examiner and declared next-turn context. The
agent receives recorded graph semantics and a bound shared scene, then proposes
eligible work through the normal command path. Layout, color and proximity
never imply warrant, causality or priority. A semantic peer offers the same
actions; optional vision capture and native screen-reader evaluation are not
A1 requirements.

A complete evidence cycle connects:

1. An inquiry/Hypothesis, target/rival and named scoped criteria.
2. Bounded evidence intake or retrieval, including contrary basis and omissions.
3. Native candidate/object examination and alternative comparison.
4. A governed Review/decision where authority changes, with its actual receipt.
5. The resulting Workspace and graph positions, distinguished from projection lag.
6. A snapshot-bound assessment with separate axes, unknowns and limitations.
7. Registered posture, open obligations and eligible next-work previews.
8. An explicit supported, challenged, insufficient, deferred or further-cycle
   disposition, as the native meaning permits. Open mandatory obligations
   prevent a satisfied closure.
9. Selective invalidation after changed/retracted basis, a retained prior
   assessment and a new examination version.

At least two connected cycles must operate through the actual owners. A fluent
answer, terminal Work status or isolated graph is insufficient.

## Execution and critical passes

For each selected item, follow its numbered construction steps. Reuse and
inspect existing registered owners before planning a new schema, endpoint or
migration. Record an identified missing contractual function before changing
its implementation; material scope changes require renewed roadmap approval.

The **execution pass** demonstrates every acceptance criterion through the
appropriate real owners. Integrated model-dependent functions use an actual
eligible model, official adapters and real persistence. Retain the exact
candidate, inputs, identities, command/test invocation, result, receipts and
coverage limits. Assemble a stable integrated candidate before the passes;
do not create a dependency-revalidation chain after every preparatory edit.
Every numbered construction step records its outcome and evidence under its
owning item. Verified existing implementation may satisfy a step; undocumented
omission may not. Do not begin a step owned by another item or an incomplete
prerequisite. Report the selected item, delivered behavior, evidence, findings
and remaining work when its passes conclude.

The **critical pass** evaluates the user's intended outcome and challenges
each criterion with distinct semantic, authority, adverse-state, recovery and
composition tests. It records the likely user failure, findings and their
disposition. Repeating execution tests or summarizing their success is not a
critical pass.

A third repair pass is necessary only when findings require changes. Material
findings cannot be waived: repair them and rerun affected execution and critical
checks on the same repaired candidate. Two consecutive unsuccessful attempts
at the same boundary stop local retries. Diagnose contract, architecture,
dependencies, environment and verification, then state a changed remedy before
resuming. Failure counters and retained evidence survive that diagnosis.
This rule includes construction and preparatory checks, not only the final
capture runner. Renaming an attempt or moving a failure into “preparation” does
not authorize another local retry loop.

The roadmap correction itself has separate
[execution](FE-A1-ROADMAP-EXECUTION-REVIEW.json) and
[critical](FE-A1-ROADMAP-CRITICAL-REVIEW.json) records. These are planning checks;
they do not close product functions or grant approval.
